## Wikipedia mentions (up to 5)

- Note for human review: this artist DOES have his own Wikipedia article, under the museum's alternate spelling — [Murry DePillars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murry_DePillars), "a Chicago-born artist and educator and a member of AfriCOBRA", dates 1938–2008, matching this docket exactly. The docket's premise (no article of his own) is wrong; the name should likely go into MANUAL_WIKI_MATCHES.
- [Wadsworth Jarrell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wadsworth_Jarrell) — listed in the AfriCOBRA painter's infobox under Patrons: "Murry N. DePillars."
- [For Alto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/For_Alto) — Anthony Braxton's solo album includes a track dedicated to him, "To Artist Murry DePillars", discussed by saxophonist Harold Land in DownBeat's Blindfold Test.
- [Virginia Commonwealth University School of the Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virginia_Commonwealth_University_School_of_the_Arts) — "Assistant Dean Murry N. DePillars became acting dean and eventually assumed the formal role of dean", and oversaw the school's growth.
- [William Walker (muralist)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Walker_(muralist)) — "Artist Murry DePillars, executive vice president of Chicago State University and a member" of the group urging artists to engage with racial problems. He also appears as an illustrator credit on [Chancellor Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chancellor_Williams) and as a participant on [James A. Porter Colloquium on African American Art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_A._Porter_Colloquium_on_African_American_Art).
